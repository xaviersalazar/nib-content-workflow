# Charlie Duke's Measles and the Apollo 13 Crew Change

## The Incident

Merely 72 hours before Apollo 13's launch, astronaut Ken Mattingly was removed from his position as Command Module Pilot and replaced by Jack Swigert from the backup crew. The reason: Charlie Duke, also from the backup team, had contracted measles from one of his children and exposed Mattingly—the only crew member from either the prime or backup team who lacked immunity to the disease.

## The Risk

Mission planners feared that if Mattingly contracted measles during the flight, he could become ill while alone in the Command Module while Jim Lovell and Fred Haise conducted lunar surface operations. This medical precaution inadvertently set the stage for the mission's survival.

## Impact on the Mission

NASA engineer Jerry Woodfill argues this seemingly unfortunate event actually saved the crew. According to Woodfill: "I think Charlie Duke's measles contributed to the rescue... the astronauts on board were perfect to deal with what happened."

Swigert's specific qualifications made him uniquely suited for the crisis:
- His athletic build better endured the Command Module's harsh cold and water rationing
- He possessed "the best knowledge of Command Module malfunction procedures" among Apollo astronauts
- His meticulous personality proved essential for accurately transcribing critical procedures during the emergency

Ironically, Mattingly later flew with Duke on Apollo 16 and contributed significantly to Apollo 13's recovery from Mission Control.
