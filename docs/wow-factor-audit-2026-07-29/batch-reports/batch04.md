# Batch Audit: batch04

## Categories covered
space, artificial-intelligence, video-games, economics, medicine, everyday-objects, household-science

## Batch summary
- Total facts reviewed: 213
- Total PASS / REVISE / BORDERLINE / REJECT: 139 / 20 / 33 / 21
- % of batch not meeting full quality bar: 74/213 = 34.7%
- Strongest categories in this batch: **medicine** (32/41 PASS, 78%) and **space** (29/46 PASS, 63%) — both have a deep bench of vivid, self-contained, highly visual facts (golden blood, dead-salmon fMRI, Yap stones, blue Martian sunsets).
- Weakest categories in this batch: **everyday-objects** (17/29 PASS, 59%, with 5 REJECT) and **economics** (22/32 PASS, 69%, with 3 REJECT) — both lean on repetitive "origin story" or "abstract systems" structures that dilute wow.
- Most common failure patterns observed:
  1. **Bureaucratic/administrative history** — facts centered on votes, definitions, patents, or committee decisions (dwarf-planet definition, income tax history, NES industry strategy) read as textbook trivia, not wonder.
  2. **"Origin story" fatigue** — an enormous share of this batch (especially AI, video games, everyday-objects, household-science) uses the same skeleton: "[Ordinary thing] was invented/discovered by accident by [person], for [surprising reason]." When 3+ facts in the same category share this exact shape, the weakest ones blur together and lose impact.
  3. **Technicality dressed as wonder** — facts where the "surprise" evaporates on a second's reflection (Voyager 2 launched before Voyager 1; asteroids are older than Earth) because the underlying claim is either definitionally true or common sense once stated.
  4. **Abstract systems/jargon** — facts requiring the reader to hold an economic, financial, or engineering system in their head (bullwhip effect, negative interest rates, three-way switch wiring, GDP) before the "aha" lands; these disproportionately fail Broad Audience Appeal for younger readers.
  5. **Serialized micro-facts** — several near-identical facts split into 2–3 entries about the same narrow story (Oort Cloud distance stats, dishwasher history, blood transfusion history) where one strong fact would serve the database better than three medium ones.
- Topics that feel oversaturated within this batch: Oort Cloud (3 facts, largely redundant scale/distance framing), blood transfusion history (3 facts), MRI (3 facts, one purely explanatory), dishwashers (3 facts telling one linear story), buttons (3 facts, only one truly strong), AI "we underestimated how hard this would be" origin stories (Dartmouth, Perceptron, computer vision — 3 facts sharing one structure).
- Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/economic/historical context: **economics** (banking mechanics, negative interest rates, bullwhip effect, GDP, income tax history all skew toward adult financial literacy) and, to a lesser extent, **artificial-intelligence** (AI-winter funding cycles, Chinese Room philosophy, AlexNet contest mechanics assume familiarity with tech-industry framing).
