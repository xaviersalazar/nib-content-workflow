# Batch Audit: batch07

## Categories covered
history, technology, cars, geography, colors, insects, strange-places

## Batch summary
- Total facts reviewed: 212
- Total PASS / REVISE / BORDERLINE / REJECT: 121 / 28 / 44 / 19
- % of batch not meeting full quality bar (REVISE+BORDERLINE+REJECT)/N: 91/212 = 42.9%
- **Strongest categories:** insects (21/27 PASS, 78%), strange-places (23/30 PASS, 77%), geography (31/44 PASS, 70%), colors (20/30 PASS, 67%) — these categories lean on vivid, visual, physically strange phenomena that need little context to land.
- **Weakest categories:** history (12/44 PASS, 27%; 13 REJECTs — the single worst category) and cars (7/23 PASS, 30%). History is dragged down by academic/procedural entries (courts, exonerations, industrial economics, "the history of X" framing). Cars is dragged down by dry engineering-spec and industry-adoption facts.
- **Most common failure patterns observed:**
  1. "The History of X" / textbook-heading hooks that name a topic instead of foregrounding the surprise (middle-ages-universities, samurai-ran-japan, valleys-carved-by-processes).
  2. Facts requiring economic, legal, political, or industrial-process background to land (industrial-revolution-illegal-export, french-revolution-american-war, printing-press-lawsuit, usb-imac-mainstream).
  3. Bodies that end on a date, statistic, or procedural note instead of a vivid final image (salem-witch-trials cluster, titanic-warning-never-reached-bridge).
  4. Hooks that are accurate but flat statements of the topic rather than the surprise itself (electric-cars-early-competitive, automobile-racing-first-true-race).
  5. Bodies that withhold or fail to deliver the payoff the hook promises (crash-test-dummies-measure-crashes never says what the "worse" alternative was).
- **Oversaturated topics within this batch:**
  - **Mongol Empire** (3 facts) — largest-contiguous is the strongest; small-armies and messenger-network are both mid-tier and cover overlapping "impressive but not amazing" ground.
  - **Salem Witch Trials** (3 facts) — all three are procedural/legalistic (accusations spread, spectral evidence banned, apology timeline) and all three failed; the underlying story needs a completely different, more visceral angle to work in Nib.
  - **Titanic** (3 facts) — lifeboat-capacity-gap is excellent; the other two are comparatively flat and largely redundant once the strongest is kept.
  - **Crash Test Dummies** (4 facts) — heaviest cluster in the batch; only "built mostly for men" clearly earns its place, the rest overlap or under-deliver.
  - **Printing Press** (3 facts) — only the Korea-before-Gutenberg angle has real potential; the lawsuit and surviving-Bibles facts are dry and redundant with each other.
  - **Salar de Uyuni** (3 facts), **Fly Geyser** (3 facts), **Door to Hell** (3 facts) — each cluster has one clear standout (mirror, accidental-origin, and explorer-rappel respectively) and two weaker/overlapping supporting facts.
  - **Electric Cars** (2 facts) and **Windshield Wipers** (2 facts) and **Car Horns** (2 facts) — small but real overlap; in each pair one fact is meaningfully stronger.
- **Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/economic/historical context:** history (French Revolution economics, Industrial Revolution IP law, printing-press courtroom history, Silk Road religious diffusion) and, to a lesser extent, cars (engineering specs like seat-belt tensile strength, CVT mechanics, industry standards-adoption stories). Geography and colors mostly avoid this trap by keeping the payoff physical/visual rather than institutional.
