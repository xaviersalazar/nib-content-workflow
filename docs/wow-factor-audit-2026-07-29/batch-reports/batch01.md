- Weakest categories in this batch (by PASS rate): food (5/18, 28%), ancient-civilizations (13/37, 35%), religion-beliefs (22/48, 46%)
- Most common failure patterns observed:
  1. **Flat, topic-label hooks** that describe the subject instead of foregrounding the surprise (e.g. "The Persians Built an Ancient Superhighway," "Indus Cities Ran on Grid Streets and Standardized Bricks") — matches the brief's "textbook heading" warning pattern.
  2. **Hedge language undermining payoff** — "may have," "the story goes," "likely," "historians debate" repeatedly soften facts right at the moment they should land hardest (phoenicians-ships, pizza-roman-no-tomatoes, infinity-symbol-wallis, dollar-sign-money-design, pirate-havens-shipwreck-clues, check-mark-origin-uncertain).
  3. **Academic/specialist jargon** requiring prior knowledge — religious-studies terms (henotheism, etiological myth, "collective effervescence"), political-science terms (principate, princeps), chemistry mechanism jargon (electron transfer, Haber-Bosch, resin codes), name/date-heavy ancient-history entries (Hittite kings, Persian/Hittite chronology).
  4. **Generic or filler body endings** that restate the hook instead of adding a new rewarding detail ("still powers countless machines today," "few people realize..."), leaving the body weaker than the hook.
  5. **Within-batch redundancy** — several symbol and topic "clusters" contain 2-4 facts recycling the same core reveal from marginally different angles, diluting distinctiveness (see below).
  6. **Abstract political/administrative/economic content** — governance, citizenship law, canon formation, trade-route economics — consistently underperforms on Broad Audience Appeal, exactly as the brief warns.
- Topics that feel oversaturated within this batch:
  - **Religious calendars** (4 facts: judaism-covenant-law-history, religious-calendars-sky-cycles, religious-calendars-different-years, religious-calendars-move-festivals) — heavy overlap, especially the "5780s" stat repeated in two facts.
  - **Prayer beads** (3 facts) — all mild etymology/list facts with similar payoff shape.
  - **Bananas** (3 facts) — modern-clones, giant-herb, gros-michel-disease all thin individually; better as one strong fact.
  - **Jolly Roger** (3 facts), **peace symbol** (3), **check-mark** (3), **dollar sign** (3), **ampersand** (3), **skull & crossbones** (3), **infinity symbol** (2) — famous-symbols is structurally built on 2-3 facts per symbol; most clusters have one clear standout and 1-2 weaker riders that should be cut or merged.
  - **Ancient astronomers without telescopes** — maya-astronomy and babylonians-astronomy are near-duplicates of the same reveal.
  - **Indus mystery** — indus-undeciphered-script and indus-mysterious-decline cover the same "we don't know" theme; the script version is stronger.
  - **Chocolate as a bitter historical drink** — appears in both ancient-civilizations (aztec-chocolate) and food (chocolate-drink-history).
- Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/economic/historical context: **religion-beliefs** (canon formation, henotheism, Van Gennep/Durkheim academic theory, Abrahamic-religions list) and **ancient-civilizations** (Hittite/Persian chronology, Roman citizenship policy, Augustus's "principate") are the clearest offenders. **Food** leans on economic/trade and nutrition-science framing (salt trade routes, rice/beriberi, potato famine) more than sensory or visual delight.
