**Strongest categories in this batch:** Architecture and Survival/Body-Limits are the clear standouts. Architecture facts consistently pair a vivid, concrete image (a floating dome, a hidden spire, a tunnel through a living tree, a bridge grown from roots) with a payoff that exceeds the hook — the category reads like it was written by people who understand Nib's voice. Survival/Body-Limits is similarly strong: nearly every fact centers on the reader's own body doing something counterintuitive, which is inherently visceral and universal.

**Weakest categories in this batch:** Astronomy is the weakest by a wide margin (over half the batch flagged), driven almost entirely by the three Cosmic Microwave Background facts and several black-hole/star facts that lean on abstract numbers (septillions, Kelvin fractions, percentages) without a grounding comparison. Business is the second weakest — not because the writing is bad, but because a meaningful fraction of the category depends on economics, finance, or corporate-history literacy (Veblen goods, price controls, stock splits, venture capital terms, patent law) that will not land with younger readers, exactly the failure pattern the audit brief calls out by name.

**Most common failure patterns observed:**
1. **Numbers without a graspable comparison** — facts that lean on a statistic (percentages, Kelvin fractions, dollar figures) instead of translating it into something visual or bodily (astronomy CMB facts, several business/finance facts).
2. **Adult/technical domain knowledge required** — economics terms (Veblen goods, price controls, unicorns), legal terms (patent pools, trademark genericide nuance), or corporate-finance concepts that a child or many teens would not have a foothold in.
3. **Hook buries the real twist in the body** — several facts (Milky Way/Andromeda collision, tree-of-oil-in-jewelry-style facts) lead with the expected, well-known claim and only reveal the genuinely surprising detail a sentence or two later.
4. **Within-batch redundancy** — clusters of 2-3 facts about the same narrow subtopic (three CMB facts, three trade-secret facts, three corpse-flower facts, three poison-ivy facts, three Stapp rocket-sled facts, three hysterical-strength facts) where one or two facts clearly outclass the others covering the same ground.
5. **"Informative but not memorable"** — facts that are accurate and mildly interesting but produce an "oh, neat" reaction rather than "wait, really?" — common across business and astronomy history facts (business-card origins, lighthouse-keeper automation, stock exchange founding).

**Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/economic/historical context:**
