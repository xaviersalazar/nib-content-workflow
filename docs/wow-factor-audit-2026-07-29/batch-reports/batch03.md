# Batch Audit: batch03

## Categories covered

## Batch summary
- Total facts reviewed: 214
- Total PASS / REVISE / BORDERLINE / REJECT: 117 / 15 / 67 / 15
- % of batch not meeting full quality bar (REVISE+BORDERLINE+REJECT)/N: 97/214 = 45.3%
- Strongest categories in this batch: **physics** (27/34 PASS, 79%) and **human-behavior** (18/29 PASS, 62%). Physics facts consistently deliver a clean, visual, one-sentence "wait, really?" moment (fridge magnet vs. gravity, golf balls on the Moon-adjacent chess-atoms comparison, astronauts constantly falling). Human-behavior facts benefit from being instantly testable/relatable (fold your arms, hold eye contact, cross your arms for pain relief).
- Weakest categories in this batch: **castles-fortresses** (10/29 PASS, 34%, with 18 BORDERLINE) and **movies** (8/19 PASS, 42%, with 4 REJECT). Castles-fortresses is dominated by procedural/mechanical facts (how a counterweight works, how a moat was dug, how a siege tower was fireproofed) that are mildly interesting but rarely produce a genuine "wow" — they read as good background information rather than shareable surprises. Movies has several facts that lean on institutional/technical trivia (IMAX film specs, Oscar ownership rules, film-festival history) rather than a vivid reveal.
- Most common failure patterns observed:
  1. **Procedural/historical facts with no single vivid payoff** — the fact explains *how something worked* rather than delivering a jaw-dropping reveal (many castles-fortresses entries, several King Arthur/Atlantis entries).
  2. **Hedged, uncertain language undermining the body** — "may," "some believe," "a popular theory holds," "reportedly" — appears repeatedly (unicorns-tapestry-fame, translation-machine-vs-cat, atlantis-from-plato, tongue-twisters-expose-speech-errors) and weakens the confidence of the payoff.
  3. **Redundancy within 3-per-topic clusters** — Yeti, Kraken, Phoenix, Loch Ness, Minotaur, Morse Code, IMAX, and Microexpressions each have 2-4 facts covering very similar ground; in every cluster one or two entries are clearly weaker restatements.
  4. **Statistic-only hooks with no visual/emotional anchor** — "12 to 70 letters," "4,000 hillforts," "500+ languages" — numbers without a comparison a reader can picture.
  5. **Corporate, administrative, or institutional context** — licence to crenellate, Oscar resale rules, UN document translation counts, Pixar/Jobs corporate history — technically true but adult/bureaucratic in flavor.
  6. **Body listing several loosely connected examples instead of one sharp idea** — loanwords list (ketchup/sugar/alcohol/shampoo), phoenix-across-cultures list, Greek-gods-behaving-badly list — violates the "one central idea" digestibility rule.
- Topics that feel oversaturated in this batch: **Loch Ness** (4 facts), **Yeti** (3), **Kraken** (3), **Phoenix** (3), **Minotaur** (3), **Morse Code** (3), **Microexpressions** (3, cluster is weak overall), **IMAX** (2, both weak/borderline), **Coraline** (2 across stop-motion).
- Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/historical context: **castles-fortresses** (sieges, Vauban's mathematics, the Maginot Line, licence to crenellate all assume adult historical/military framing) and pockets of **myths-legends** (King Arthur literary history, Norse source criticism) and **languages** (Shakespearean "original pronunciation," the UN human-rights document).
