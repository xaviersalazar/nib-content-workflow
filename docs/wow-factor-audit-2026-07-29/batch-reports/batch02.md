- Strongest categories in this batch: **sleep-dreams** (8% not meeting bar) and **human-civilization** (10%). Both benefit from subjects that are inherently visual, extreme, or paradoxical (deep-sea biology, sleep-science paradoxes), which naturally clear the wow bar even with fairly plain writing.
- Weakest categories in this batch: **animals** (34% not meeting bar) and **aviation** (27%). Literature and animals both contain clusters of 2-3 facts on the same sub-topic (e.g., three penguin facts, three platypus facts, three axolotl facts, three mantis-shrimp facts, three coin facts, three Roman-roads facts) where the second or third entry adds little beyond what the strongest fact in the cluster already delivers.
- Most common failure patterns observed:
  1. **Redundant sub-clusters** — many topics (penguins, platypus, axolotls, mantis shrimp, chameleons, giant squid, sleep paralysis, sleepwalking, hot-air balloons, Roman roads/sewers/coins) have 2-3 facts covering the same core revelation from slightly different angles. The strongest one usually earns PASS; the others frequently repeat the mechanism with less vivid specifics and should be considered for removal per the redundancy rule.
  2. **Hook makes a promise the body doesn't keep** — several REVISE cases (ravens-solve-problems-together, aerodynamics-pressure-lift) have strong headlines that the body fails to pay off with a concrete detail or resolution.
  3. **Chronological/biographical writing** — a handful of facts (gliders-lilienthal-human-flight, ejection-seats-pilot-safety-origin, flying-boats-first-powered-seaplane, libraries-changing-role) read like encyclopedia or corporate-history entries: dates and names presented in order rather than organized around a surprise.
  4. **Ungrounded statistics** — numbers given without a relatable comparison (flight-recorders' "3,400 g and 1,100°C", ocean-currents' "eight knots") stay abstract instead of vivid.
  5. **Requires prior knowledge the reader may not have** — a few hooks only work if the reader already knows a reference figure (laws-written-codes assumes familiarity with Hammurabi; epic-poems-oral-formulas assumes the reader already finds "Homer" mythologized).
  6. **Administrative/bureaucratic/corporate framing** — a small set of facts center on tax systems, library archiving projects, or company founding stories, which the audit brief explicitly flags as skewing adult and dry.
- Topics that feel oversaturated (too many similar facts in this batch):
  - **Penguins** (3 facts: fathers-incubate, winter-breeding, creches) — winter-breeding is largely a restatement of fathers-incubate.
  - **Platypus** (3 facts) — soft-sensor-bill is a weak echo of the much stronger venom and gravel-chewing facts.
  - **Axolotls** (3 facts) — the Aztec-name fact is trivia riding on the coattails of the much stronger regeneration/neoteny facts.
  - **Mantis shrimp** (3 facts) — all excellent subjects, but complex-eyes is markedly more jargon-heavy than the other two.
  - **Chameleons** (3 facts) — see-through-skin-layers duplicates the "why chameleons change color" territory of chameleons-not-just-camouflage.
  - **Giant squid** (3 facts) — filmed-2012 is a science-history footnote next to the much stronger dinner-plate-eyes and sperm-whale-scars facts.
  - **Sleep paralysis / sleepwalking** (2 facts each) — in both pairs, the second fact is a flatter, more "advice-column" restatement of the first.
  - **Roman infrastructure** (human-civilization has 3 roads facts, 3 sewer facts, and 3 coin facts, all Rome-adjacent) — individually fine, but the category leans heavily on Rome as a well running dry by the third or fourth entry.
  - **"Longest X in the world" framing** — appears twice in literature (longest poem, longest novel); the novel version is redundant and weaker.
- Categories relying too heavily on adult/technical/economic/historical context:
  - **Human-civilization** is the most exposed here: taxation (irrigation-hydraulic-civilization), legal-history comparisons that assume prior knowledge (laws-written-codes), and several Rome-centric infrastructure facts skew toward "impressive to history buffs" rather than universally visceral.
  - **Aviation** has a cluster of corporate/engineering-history facts (ejection-seats-pilot-safety-origin, gliders-wright-control, flying-boats-first-powered-seaplane) that read like trade-press retrospectives.
  - **Literature** has a few facts (banned-books-history-old, epic-poems-oral-formulas, laws-adjacent history) that lean on named historical/philosophical figures the target audience may not recognize.
  - Sleep-dreams and ocean-life were the most consistently accessible categories — almost no entries required outside context to land.
